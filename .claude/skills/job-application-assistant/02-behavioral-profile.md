## Overview
Tony has not taken a formal behavioral assessment yet. Self-assessed, he is a **deliberate, detail-oriented, initiative-driven** worker who is most energized by stepping up and owning problems, and who explicitly values clarity, honesty, and kindness in the people he works with and for.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Decision-making pace | Deliberate | Prefers to think things through carefully rather than decide quickly; wants to build faster intuition over time but this isn't his default mode yet |
| Structure preference | Flexible / low-preference | Comfortable working independently, paired, in structured process, or ad hoc - the deciding factor is clear communication, not the format itself |
| Initiative | High | Most energized by stepping up and taking ownership rather than waiting to be assigned work |
| Culture sensitivity | High | Explicitly values clear, honest, kind, leadership-oriented cultures; cited Koch's Principle Based Management (PBM) culture as a strong personal fit |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Ownership of stalled/messy work:** picked up a 4-year-old, half-finished documentation migration project at Koch and drove it forward
- **Cross-functional coordination:** worked directly with software engineers, DBAs, data architects, product owners, and business stakeholders in both internship roles
- **Detail orientation:** deliberate decision-making style paired with hands-on data cleaning/cross-referencing work

## How You Work Best
- In cultures that are clear, honest, kind, and leadership-oriented (Koch's PBM culture is the explicit positive reference point)
- With any team structure (independent, paired, structured, or ad hoc), as long as expectations and communication are clear
- On problems with visible, tangible impact where his contribution and influence are traceable

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Working with difficult supervisors/colleagues:** frame as active self-improvement - Tony has identified this himself and wants to get better at navigating interpersonal friction, not avoiding it
- **Building faster decision-making and intuition:** frame as a deliberate, thorough decision-maker who is consciously developing more speed and instinct as he gains experience, not someone who is slow to decide

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "clear expectations", "honest/transparent culture", "ownership", "take initiative", "cross-functional collaboration", "growth path", "autonomy"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "fast-paced, ambiguous environment", "figure it out as you go", "high-pressure decision-making" - not a hard no, but worth surfacing since Tony's default mode is deliberate rather than fast, and he's still building comfort with ambiguity

## Management Style Preferences
- Works well under leadership that communicates clearly and treats people with honesty and kindness (PBM-style principle-based management is a strong positive signal)
- Vague expectations, messy/unstructured workflows, and difficult-to-work-with supervisors are draining - not disqualifying, but worth probing in interviews

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** lean on the ownership-of-stalled-work story (Koch documentation migration) and the cross-functional coordination angle rather than claiming fast-paced/high-ambiguity comfort he doesn't yet have
- **CV:** emphasize initiative and ownership language in bullets ("picked up," "drove forward," "coordinated across")
- **Interviews:** use the Koch documentation migration and Agile sprint facilitation examples for ownership/initiative questions; be ready to speak honestly about the interpersonal-friction growth area if asked about weaknesses
- **Don't overstate:** don't claim fast, instinctive decision-making or comfort with high ambiguity - both are explicitly still growth areas, not current strengths
